I

About

I am an undergraduate in the joint programme between Harbin Engineering University and the University of Southampton, reading Automation on the Chinese side and BEng Control Engineering on the UK side, with a current average of 88.13/100 and an expected First Class Honours.

My research interest started with a simple question: what happens when a robot's actuator quietly loses authority and nobody notices. That led to a first-author paper on bipedal balance recovery using a nonlinear disturbance observer paired with reinforcement learning, and a second one on Q-learning based path planning for warehouse robots. Both share the same instinct, that a machine should be able to reason about its own degradation and still finish the task.

I am currently completing my undergraduate thesis on LiDAR-based SLAM mapping and autonomous navigation for mobile robots, building the full pipeline from point cloud registration to costmap-based navigation in simulation. Alongside research I have worked on warehouse robot test platforms at Hai Robotics, on FPV drone hardware, and on the Huawei Kunpeng and Ascend AI stack, which keeps my modelling grounded in things that actually have to boot up.

Publications

01

Fault-Tolerant Bipedal Balance Recovery via Nonlinear Disturbance Observer and Reinforcement Learning-Based Torque Redistribution

ICSP 2026, 11th International Conference on Intelligent Computing and Signal Processing, Hefei, China · IEEE Xplore, EI Compendex, Scopus First author

A hybrid framework that keeps a bipedal robot standing after its ankle actuators partially fail. A nonlinear disturbance observer estimates the lost torque in real time, and a reinforcement learning policy redistributes the remaining authority across joints.

Torque loss tolerated 56% Balance survival 82% Centroid deviation 0.355 ± 0.102 m Outperforms mainstream benchmarks
02

Three-stage Path Planning for Warehouse Robots Based on Q-learning Algorithm

AMCCE 2024, 5th International Conference on Automation, Mechanical Control and Computational Engineering, Athens, Greece · Highlights in Science, Engineering and Technology (ISSN 2791-0210) First author

A MATLAB warehouse simulation and a three-stage planning scheme that decouples global path planning from local obstacle avoidance, addressing the coupling problem that makes classical planners stall in dense storage layouts.

Faster convergence than baselines Higher navigation accuracy Iterative parameter tuning
